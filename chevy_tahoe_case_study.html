Interactive Case Study: The $1 Chevy Tahoe

Interactive Activity (annotated chat) · Estimated time: 10 minutes

In December 2023, someone convinced a car dealership's AI chatbot to "agree" to sell a brand-new SUV for a single dollar. The exchange went viral — more than 20 million views — and it's now one of the most famous real-world examples of an LLM attack.

Here's what makes it the perfect first case study: the customer didn't hack anything. They just typed the right words in the right order. In this activity you'll look at the real conversation and uncover exactly how each message did its work.

💡
A bit of context: models come with instructions.

The chatbot on that dealership's website was not a raw, general-purpose model. Like most AI tools built for a real job, it had been given its own set of behind-the-scenes instructions, telling it something like "you are a helpful assistant for this dealership, answer questions about our vehicles, and stay on topic." These hidden instructions are called a system prompt, and they are how a company turns a general model into a chatbot for one specific purpose.

Here is the catch. Those instructions are written in plain language, the same plain language the customer types in. The model does not get a special, protected channel for the company's rules and a separate one for the customer's messages. It reads all of it as text. That is exactly the seam this "customer" pried open, and it is worth keeping in mind as you read the conversation.
Before you explore

Read the conversation once, top to bottom, and take a guess. Why do you think this worked? What did this "customer" do that an ordinary customer never would? Hold onto your answer — then reveal the annotations and see how close you were.
